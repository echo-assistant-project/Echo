# Phone photography

## 1. Light is everything
Soft daylight beats everything: shoot near a window or outdoors in shade, or in the hour after sunrise/before sunset. Avoid harsh noon sun on faces and mixed light (tube light + daylight) indoors. Turn off the flash for most shots.

## 2. Clean the lens, hold steady
Wipe the lens (it's usually smudged), hold with two hands, elbows in, tap to focus, and press gently. Use a timer or a cheap tripod for low light.

## 3. Composition
Turn on the grid; place the subject on a third line, not always in the centre. Keep horizons straight. Fill the frame; remove clutter from the background. Shoot from the subject's eye level (children, pets) or try a lower angle for drama. Leading lines (roads, walls) pull the eye in.

## 4. Exposure
After tapping to focus, slide the sun/brightness icon down a little to keep skies and white walls from burning out. Use HDR for high-contrast scenes.

## 5. Specific shots
- **Portraits**: portrait mode, face toward window light, background far behind, use 2x lens (less distortion).
- **Food/products**: daylight from the side, plain background, top-down for flat items, 45° for drinks and burgers, clean props.
- **Property interiors**: all lights on and curtains open, wide lens held at chest height, vertical lines straight, shoot from corners, tidy every surface. Exteriors in morning light.
- **Night**: night mode, keep still for the count, avoid digital zoom.

## 6. Quick editing (Snapseed / built-in editor)
Straighten and crop, small brightness/contrast tweak, a little warmth, remove a stray object (Magic Eraser/Healing). Don't over-saturate; keep property photos honest.

## 7. Share and store
Back up to Google Photos/OneDrive, keep originals, export at full resolution for listings, and ask permission before posting photos of people.
