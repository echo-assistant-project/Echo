# Fitness for beginners

Check with a doctor first if you have heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, joint problems, are pregnant, or are over 60 and new to exercise. Stop if you feel chest pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness.

## 1. The weekly target (WHO)
Adults: 150–300 minutes of moderate activity a week (brisk walking — you can talk but not sing), or 75–150 minutes vigorous, plus muscle-strengthening on 2+ days. Older adults: add balance exercises 3 days a week. Something is always better than nothing.

## 2. Weeks 1–4: walk
Start with 10–15 minutes daily, add 5 minutes each week, reach 30 minutes brisk. Walk after meals (helps blood sugar). Step goal: build from your current average by ~1,000 a week.

## 3. Home strength (no equipment), 2–3 times a week
Squats to a chair · wall or knee push-ups · glute bridges · lunges holding a wall · plank on knees · calf raises · bird-dog. 2 rounds of 8–12 slow repetitions, rest 60 seconds. Make it harder by adding reps, rounds or a water bottle as a weight.

## 4. Flexibility and balance
5 minutes after workouts: hamstring, calf, chest, shoulder and hip stretches held 20–30 seconds. Balance: stand on one leg near a wall, heel-to-toe walking. Yoga and tai chi are excellent for both.

## 5. Running (optional)
Couch-to-5K style: alternate 1 minute jogging and 90 seconds walking for 20 minutes, 3 days a week; slowly lengthen the jogging parts over 9 weeks.

## 6. Staying consistent
Same time each day, put it in the calendar (Echo can remind you), track it (a habit streak), find a partner, and plan for bad days ("if it rains, 15 minutes of home workout"). Progress slowly — about 10% more each week — to avoid injury.

## 7. For older adults and sore knees
Chair exercises, water walking, cycling, leg raises and step-ups on a low step; warm up longer; good shoes; pain that lasts over a day after exercise means scale back.
