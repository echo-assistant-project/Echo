# Property investment math

Echo's tools do the arithmetic: "loan EMI for 40 lakh at 8.5% for 20 years", "compare loans", "CAGR", "inflation adjust".

## 1. True total cost
Price + stamp duty and registration + GST (under construction) + legal + interiors + brokerage = **all-in cost**. Yields and returns must use this, not the sticker price.

## 2. EMI and affordability
EMI = P·r·(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1), with r = annual rate ÷ 12 and n = months. Keep all EMIs under ~35–40% of take-home pay, keep 6 months of EMI as emergency fund, and compare banks by total interest paid, not just the rate. Prepaying early in the loan saves the most interest.

## 3. Rental yield
Gross yield = annual rent ÷ all-in cost. Net yield = (annual rent − maintenance − property tax − repairs − vacancy of about 1 month) ÷ all-in cost. Indian residential net yields are often around 2–4%; compare that with loan interest and FD rates honestly.

## 4. Cash flow
Monthly rent − EMI − maintenance − tax. Negative cash flow means you pay every month and depend on appreciation — say this plainly.

## 5. Appreciation
Use the area's real past growth (registered prices, not brochure claims), compute CAGR = (end ÷ start)^(1/years) − 1, and subtract inflation for the real gain. Infrastructure news (capital city, highways, metro) can drive growth but timelines slip — treat projections as scenarios: slow / expected / fast.

## 6. Buy vs rent
Compare over 10+ years: buying (down payment's lost returns + interest + costs − rent saved − appreciation) vs renting (rent + investing the down payment and EMI difference). Buying wins with long stays, stable income and reasonable prices; renting wins with mobility and high price-to-rent ratios.

## 7. Risk checklist
Liquidity (property takes months to sell), concentration (too much of your wealth in one asset), legal title risk (see property-buying-due-diligence), and leverage. Not financial advice — for big decisions, consult a SEBI-registered adviser.
