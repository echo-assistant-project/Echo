# Better sleep

## 1. How much
Adults 7–9 hours, teenagers 8–10, children more. Older adults sleep lighter but still need 7–8.

## 2. The anchor: a fixed wake-up time
Same wake time every day, weekends included. Get daylight within an hour of waking. This sets the body clock more than bedtime does. Echo's "bedtime for" and "wake times for" tools plan around 90-minute cycles.

## 3. Wind down (last hour)
Dim lights, screens away (or night mode, low brightness), a warm bath, reading, light stretching, writing tomorrow's to-do list to clear the mind.

## 4. The bedroom
Cool, dark (curtains or eye mask), quiet (fan noise or earplugs), bed only for sleep and intimacy.

## 5. Daytime habits
No caffeine (tea, coffee, colas) after about 2 pm; no heavy meals or alcohol close to bed; exercise in the day but not intensely right before sleep; short naps only (20 minutes, before 3 pm).

## 6. Can't fall asleep?
If awake for about 20 minutes, get up, do something calm in dim light, return when sleepy. Don't watch the clock. Slow breathing (breathe in 4, out 6–8) or a body scan helps. Persistent insomnia responds well to CBT-I (a structured therapy) — ask a doctor.

## 7. See a doctor if
Loud snoring with pauses in breathing or gasping (possible sleep apnea), falling asleep while driving, insomnia for over 3 months, restless legs, or low mood with sleep problems (see stress-and-wellbeing).
