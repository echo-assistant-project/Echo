# Resume and job search

## 1. Target first
Pick 1–2 role titles. Collect 10 job ads for them and note repeated skills and keywords — your resume must show those (honestly).

## 2. Resume that works
- 1 page (freshers) to 2 pages (experienced). Clean format, simple fonts, PDF, no photos/tables that confuse screening software (ATS).
- Top: name, phone, email, city, LinkedIn/portfolio. A 2–3 line summary for the target role.
- Experience: role, company, dates; 3–5 bullets each in **action + task + result with numbers** ("Cut invoice processing time 40% by automating Excel reports").
- Skills relevant to the role; education; certifications; projects (essential for freshers).
- Tailor the summary and top bullets for each application. No spelling errors — read it aloud.

## 3. LinkedIn
Professional photo, headline with role and value ("Data Analyst | Excel, SQL, Power BI"), About section in first person, experience matching the resume, skills, recommendations, and "Open to work" (recruiters only if you're employed).

## 4. Where to look
Company career pages, LinkedIn, Naukri, Indeed, NCS portal (government), campus/alumni groups, and referrals — a referral is the single best way to get an interview. Ask specifically: "Could you refer me for the [role] opening, ID ___? Here's my resume."

## 5. Applying
Quality over quantity: 5 tailored applications beat 50 generic ones. A short cover message: why this company, the 2 most relevant achievements, a call to action. Track every application (company, role, date, contact, status, follow-up date).

## 6. Not getting calls?
Check keywords against the ads, quantify results, apply within the first days of a posting, use referrals, build a small portfolio project, and get one honest reviewer.

## 7. Career change
Find transferable skills, take one focused course with a project, start with adjacent roles, and network with people already in the field (short informational chats).
