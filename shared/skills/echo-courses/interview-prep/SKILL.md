# Interview preparation

## 1. Research (1 hour)
What the company does, recent news, products, competitors, the job description's top 5 requirements, the interviewer's background (LinkedIn). Prepare one sentence on why you want *this* company.

## 2. "Tell me about yourself" (60–90 seconds)
Present (current role and a strength) → past (relevant experience with one achievement) → future (why this role is the next step). Practise until natural, not memorised.

## 3. STAR stories (prepare 6)
**S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction (what *you* did), **R**esult (numbers). Cover: an achievement, a challenge/failure and the lesson, conflict with a colleague, leadership or initiative, a tight deadline, learning something fast. Reuse them for many questions.

## 4. Common questions
Strengths (with proof), weakness (a real one plus what you're doing about it), why leave your job (positive, forward-looking), where in 5 years, why should we hire you (match to their needs), gaps in the resume (brief, honest).

## 5. Technical and case rounds
Revise fundamentals for the role, think aloud, ask clarifying questions, and say what you'd do to verify your answer. For coding interviews see coding-interview-prep.

## 6. Salary and HR
Research the market range; let them share the range first if possible; give a researched number or range; negotiate after the offer (see negotiation). Ask about notice period, joining date, benefits, work mode.

## 7. On the day
Online: test camera, mic and internet 15 minutes early, quiet room, neutral background, look at the camera. In person: arrive 10 minutes early, copies of resume. Ask 2–3 good questions ("What does success look like in the first 90 days?"). Send a short thank-you email the same day.

## 8. Mock interview mode
Ask one question at a time, wait for the answer, then give specific feedback: structure, relevance, evidence, length, confidence — and a better version of one sentence.
