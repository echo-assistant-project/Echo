# Public speaking

## 1. One message
Finish this sentence: "After my talk, the audience will ___." Every story and slide must serve it. Know your audience: what they care about, what they already know, how long they'll listen.

## 2. Structure
**Opening** (first 30 seconds): a question, a surprising fact, or a short story — not "Good morning, my name is…" at length. **Body**: 3 points maximum, each with an example or story. **Close**: repeat the message and the action you want. Signpost: "First… second… finally…".

## 3. Write for the ear
Short sentences, concrete examples, numbers rounded and compared ("as big as 10 cricket grounds"). Speak from bullet notes, not a full script.

## 4. Voice and body
Slow down (most nervous speakers rush), pause before and after key points, vary pitch, speak to the back row. Stand steady, open hands, look at one person per sentence, smile at the start.

## 5. Nerves
Nervousness is normal energy. Rehearse aloud 3+ times (once recorded), arrive early, breathe slowly (in 4, out 6), focus on helping the audience rather than on yourself. Mistakes are rarely noticed — keep going.

## 6. Q&A
Repeat or rephrase the question, answer briefly, bridge back to your message. "I don't know, I'll find out and email you" is a strong answer.

## 7. Short formats
Toast/wedding speech (2–3 minutes): who you are, one warm story, wish, raise a glass. Meeting point: conclusion first, one reason, the ask. Self-introduction: name, role, one memorable detail.
