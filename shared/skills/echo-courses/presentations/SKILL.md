# Presentations

## 1. Story before slides
Write, in one sentence, what the audience should believe or do at the end. Then outline: **problem → why it matters → solution → proof → ask/next step**. Draft the headline of each slide first; if the headlines alone tell the story, the deck works.

## 2. One idea per slide
- Headline = the message ("Rental demand in Amaravati grew 18% this year"), not a topic ("Demand").
- At most ~30 words of text; the rest is said aloud. Big fonts (titles 32+, body 20+).
- One visual: a photo, a simple chart, a diagram or a single number.

## 3. Design rules
Use the theme (Design tab) and slide master for consistency; 1–2 fonts; 2–3 colours with one accent for what matters; align things (Arrange → Align); plenty of empty space; no clip-art or heavy animations (Fade/Morph only).

## 4. Charts and numbers
One chart per slide, highlight the bar/line that matters, label directly instead of legends, round numbers (₹2.4 crore, not ₹2,39,87,123).

## 5. Common decks
- **Pitch deck (10–12 slides)**: problem, solution, market, product, business model, traction, competition, team, financials, ask.
- **Status update (5)**: summary, progress, risks, decisions needed, next steps.
- **Training (any)**: goal, steps with screenshots, practice, recap.

## 6. Delivery
Rehearse aloud twice with a timer (about 1–2 minutes per slide). Use Presenter View for notes. Start with the point, end with the ask, keep 10 minutes for questions. Export a PDF for sharing.
