# English fluency

## 1. How to correct (as a tutor)
Praise what's right, fix one or two important things at a time, show the corrected sentence, give a one-line reason, and ask them to say it again. Never mock accents — clarity matters, not a "foreign" accent.

## 2. Grammar that matters most
Tenses: simple present for habits ("I work in Guntur"), present continuous for now ("I'm working on it"), present perfect for experience/up to now ("I have lived here for five years"), simple past for finished time ("I met him yesterday"). Articles: a/an for one of many, the for a specific one. Subject–verb agreement ("He goes", "They go").

## 3. Common Indian-English slips (and fixes)
"Do the needful" → "Please take care of this." · "Revert back" → "Reply". · "I am having two brothers" → "I have two brothers." · "He is knowing" → "He knows." · "Discuss about" → "Discuss". · "Since five years" → "For five years". · "Myself Ravi" → "I'm Ravi." · "Prepone" is understood in India but say "bring forward" internationally. · "Pass out" (graduate) → "graduated".

## 4. Vocabulary
Learn words in phrases ("make a decision", "take a break"), 5 new phrases a day, use each in a sentence the same day, review with spaced repetition after 1, 3, 7 days.

## 5. Speaking practice
Shadowing: play a short clip (BBC/British Council), pause, repeat with the same rhythm. Daily 10-minute self-talk about your day. Record yourself weekly. Practise conversations with Echo: Echo asks, you answer, Echo corrects gently.

## 6. Pronunciation tips
Word stress (PHO-to-graph, pho-TO-gra-pher), "v" vs "w", "th" sounds, not adding extra vowels ("school" not "eschool"), and ending sounds (-ed, -s).

## 7. Writing
Short sentences, one idea per sentence, read it aloud, use the business-writing skill for emails.
