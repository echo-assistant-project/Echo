# Bookkeeping and GST basics (India)

## 1. Separate money first
A separate bank account and UPI for the business. Every rupee in or out goes through it or is recorded on the same day.

## 2. What to record
Date, party, description, amount, GST amount, payment mode, invoice/bill number, category (sales, rent, salaries, purchases, travel, utilities). Keep every bill — photo it immediately into a folder by month.

## 3. The three reports that matter
- **Profit & Loss** (a period): income − expenses = profit.
- **Balance Sheet** (a date): what you own (cash, stock, receivables, equipment) = what you owe (loans, payables) + owner's capital.
- **Cash flow**: money actually in and out — profitable businesses still fail when cash runs out. Chase receivables weekly.

## 4. GST in plain words (check current rules on the GST portal)
- Registration is required above the turnover threshold (commonly ₹40 lakh for goods / ₹20 lakh for services, lower in some states), for inter-state supply of goods, e-commerce sellers, and some other cases. Composition scheme suits small traders with a flat low rate but no input credit.
- **GST invoice** must show: your GSTIN, invoice number and date, customer name (and GSTIN for B2B), HSN/SAC code, taxable value, GST rate, CGST+SGST (same state) or IGST (other state), total.
- **Input Tax Credit (ITC)**: GST you paid on business purchases can reduce GST you owe on sales — only if the supplier filed their return and the bill is in your GSTR-2B.
- Returns: typically GSTR-1 (sales) and GSTR-3B (summary and payment) monthly or quarterly (QRMP). Late filing means late fees and interest.

## 5. Month-end routine (1 hour)
Match bank statement to records, list unpaid invoices and follow up, check GSTR-2B against purchase bills, file returns on time, back up the books.

## 6. Tools and help
A spreadsheet works for tiny businesses; Tally, Zoho Books or Vyapar for more. Use a CA for registration, income tax and anything unclear — Echo explains, it doesn't replace professional advice.
