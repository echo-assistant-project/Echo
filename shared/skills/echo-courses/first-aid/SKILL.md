# First aid

**In any serious emergency: call 112 (or 108 for an ambulance in India) first, or ask someone to call while you help.** A hands-on course teaches this far better than reading — take one.

## 1. Check safety, response, breathing
Make sure the area is safe. Tap shoulders and shout. If no response, open the airway (tilt head, lift chin) and look/listen for normal breathing for up to 10 seconds.

## 2. Not breathing normally: CPR
Call for help and an AED (defibrillator) if one is near. Push hard and fast in the centre of the chest: 5–6 cm deep, 100–120 per minute ("Stayin' Alive" tempo), let the chest come back up. Trained: 30 compressions then 2 breaths; untrained: hands-only continuous compressions. Use an AED as soon as it arrives and follow its voice. Don't stop until help takes over or the person breathes.

## 3. Choking
Adult/child coughing: encourage coughing. Can't cough or breathe: 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades, then 5 abdominal thrusts (stand behind, fist above navel, pull in and up). Repeat. Infant: face down on your forearm, 5 back blows, then 5 chest thrusts with two fingers. Unresponsive: start CPR.

## 4. Heart attack and stroke
Heart attack: chest pain/pressure, spreading to arm, jaw or back, sweating, breathlessness → call 108/112, sit them down, loosen clothing; if not allergic and advised by the emergency line, aspirin to chew.
Stroke — **FAST**: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech slurred, Time to call. Note the time symptoms started.

## 5. Bleeding, burns, fractures
Bleeding: firm direct pressure with a clean cloth for 10+ minutes, raise the limb, don't remove soaked cloths — add more. Burns: cool running water for 20 minutes, remove rings, cover loosely with cling film; no ice, butter, toothpaste. Fractures: keep still, support the limb as found, don't straighten it.

## 6. Other emergencies
Fainting: lay them down, raise legs. Seizure: protect the head, clear the area, nothing in the mouth, time it; recovery position after; call if over 5 minutes. **Snake bite**: keep calm and still, remove rings, immobilise the limb at heart level, no cutting/sucking/tight tourniquet, rush to a hospital with anti-venom. Heat stroke: shade, cool water on skin, fan, sips of water if awake. Poisoning: call for help with the container; don't induce vomiting.

## 7. Recovery position and kit
Breathing but unresponsive: on their side, top leg bent, head tilted back. Kit: gloves, sterile dressings, bandages, tape, antiseptic, scissors, thermometer, ORS, paracetamol, emergency numbers card.
