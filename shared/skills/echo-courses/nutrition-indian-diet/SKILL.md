# Nutrition with Indian food

General guidance for healthy adults. Pregnancy, kidney disease, diabetes on medication or other conditions need a doctor or registered dietitian.

## 1. The balanced plate
Half the plate vegetables and fruits, a quarter whole grains or millets (ragi, jowar, bajra, brown rice, whole-wheat roti), a quarter protein (dal, chana, rajma, curd, paneer, eggs, fish, chicken), plus a little healthy fat. India's 2024 dietary guidelines stress variety across food groups, whole grains and millets, protein from natural foods, and limiting oil, sugar and salt.

## 2. Protein without powders
Combine cereal + pulse (rice-dal, roti-chana) through the day; add curd, milk, eggs, sprouts, peanuts, soya. The guidelines advise against relying on protein supplements; regular food is enough for most people.

## 3. Cut the big three
- **Salt**: under about 5 g a day — less pickle, papad, packaged snacks.
- **Sugar**: sweets and sugary drinks are occasional, not daily; watch "healthy" juices and biscuits.
- **Oil**: measure it (2–3 spoons per person per day), rotate oils, avoid reheated frying oil and trans fats (vanaspati).

## 4. Read labels
Check serving size, sugar, sodium and saturated fat per serving; ingredients are listed most to least — sugar or maida first is a warning. Ultra-processed packaged foods should be rare.

## 5. Weight loss that lasts
A modest calorie deficit, protein and vegetables at every meal, fewer liquid calories, regular meal times, sleep 7–8 hours, daily activity (see fitness-beginners). Aim for 0.5 kg a week, not crash diets.

## 6. Diabetes-friendly eating (with your doctor's plan)
Smaller portions of rice/roti, more vegetables and protein first in the meal, whole fruits instead of juice, millets and pulses, no sugary drinks, regular timing with medicines, check sugar as advised.

## 7. Water, children, elders
Water through the day (more in heat); children need milk/curd, fruits, eggs or pulses and less junk food; elders need protein, calcium, vitamin B12/D (ask the doctor about tests) and soft, easy-to-chew options.
