# RTI application (Right to Information Act, 2005)

## 1. What RTI can do
Any Indian citizen can ask a public authority for information it holds: file notings, status of an application, copies of documents, reasons for a decision, expenditure. It's for information that exists — not opinions or "why didn't you do X" demands.

## 2. Write a good RTI
- Addressed to the **Public Information Officer (PIO)** of the specific office.
- Short, numbered questions asking for specific records: "Provide the daily progress/status of my application no. ___ dated ___, with copies of file notings." "Name and designation of the officer currently holding the file."
- Your name and address for reply; no need to give reasons.

## 3. Fee and filing
Central departments: ₹10 fee, file online on **rtionline.gov.in** (pay online). State departments: follow the state's RTI rules and portal (fee usually ₹10 by IPO/DD/court stamp or online). BPL card holders are exempt. Copies may cost ₹2 per page.

## 4. Timelines
Reply within **30 days** (48 hours if it concerns life or liberty). If transferred to another office, the clock runs from receipt there.

## 5. First appeal
No reply in 30 days or an unsatisfactory/refused answer → First Appeal to the **First Appellate Authority** (senior officer of the same office) within 30 days. Explain briefly what was missing.

## 6. Second appeal / complaint
Not satisfied after the first appeal → Second Appeal to the **Central or State Information Commission** within 90 days. Commissions can order disclosure and penalise PIOs for unjustified delay.

## 7. Tips
Keep copies and proof of posting/online receipt; one subject per application; avoid personal information about others (exempt); be polite and precise — it gets results faster.
