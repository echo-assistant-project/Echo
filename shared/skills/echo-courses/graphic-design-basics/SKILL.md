# Graphic design basics

## 1. One message, one focal point
Decide the single thing people must notice (the offer, the date, the face). Make it the biggest and highest-contrast element. Everything else supports it.

## 2. Hierarchy
Three levels are enough: headline (big), key details (medium), small print (small). Group related items; separate unrelated ones with space.

## 3. The four principles (CRAP)
**Contrast** (size, colour, weight to show importance), **Repetition** (same fonts/colours/styles throughout), **Alignment** (everything lines up to an edge or centre — use guides), **Proximity** (related things close together).

## 4. Colour
A palette of 2–3 colours: a main brand colour, a neutral, and one accent for calls to action. Check text contrast (dark text on light background is safest). Use colour tools (Canva palette generator, coolors.co).

## 5. Fonts
Two fonts at most: one for headings, one for body. Readable sizes (on posters, the headline should read from 3 metres). Avoid stretching text, too many effects, or all caps for long lines.

## 6. White space
Empty space is not wasted — it makes designs look premium and easy to read. Margins of at least 5% on all sides.

## 7. Common formats (sizes)
Instagram post 1080×1080 / portrait 1080×1350, Story/Reel cover 1080×1920, A4 flyer 210×297 mm with 3 mm bleed for printing, YouTube thumbnail 1280×720, WhatsApp status 1080×1920.

## 8. Logos and branding
Simple, works in one colour and at tiny sizes, no clip-art; make a one-page brand sheet (logo, colours with codes, fonts) and use it everywhere. Use only images and fonts you have rights to.
