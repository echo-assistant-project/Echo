# Insurance basics (India)

## 1. Insurance is protection, not investment
Buy insurance to survive a disaster you can't pay for yourself. Keep investing separate — policies that mix the two (endowment, money-back, many ULIPs) usually give low returns and too little cover.

## 2. Term life insurance (if anyone depends on your income)
Cover of about 10–15× annual income plus loans; until retirement age; pure term from a company with a high claim settlement ratio; disclose health and habits honestly (non-disclosure = rejected claims); add your nominee and tell them.

## 3. Health insurance (everyone)
Family floater ₹10 lakh+ in big cities, plus a **super top-up** for cheaply raising cover. Check: room-rent limits, co-payment, waiting periods for pre-existing diseases, network hospitals near you, restoration benefit, no-claim bonus. Buy parents' cover early — premiums and refusals rise with age. Employer cover ends with the job; keep your own.

## 4. Other covers
Motor: third-party is compulsory; comprehensive with zero-depreciation for new cars. Home: cheap protection against fire, flood and theft. Personal accident and critical illness: useful extras, not substitutes.

## 5. Reading a policy (the parts that matter)
Sum insured, exclusions, waiting periods, sub-limits, co-pay, claim process, free-look period (to cancel within days of receiving the policy).

## 6. Making a claim
Inform the insurer/TPA quickly (cashless: before planned admission, within 24 hours for emergencies), keep all bills, discharge summary and reports, follow up in writing. If rejected unfairly: insurer's grievance cell → **Bima Bharosa** (IRDAI) → Insurance Ombudsman.

## 7. Avoid
Buying from someone who only shows "returns", agents rushing you, cancelling old health policies (you lose waiting-period credit), and hiding medical history.
