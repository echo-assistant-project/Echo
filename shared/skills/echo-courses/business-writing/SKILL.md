# Business writing

## 1. Before writing
Who reads it, what they already know, and the one action you want. If you can't say the point in one sentence, you aren't ready to write.

## 2. Structure: bottom line up front
1. **Subject line** that says the point ("Approval needed: ₹1.2 lakh for new laptops by Friday").
2. **First sentence**: the request or conclusion.
3. **Context**: only what's needed to act, in short paragraphs or bullets.
4. **Next step**: who does what by when.

## 3. Plain language rules
Short sentences (15–20 words), active voice ("We will send" not "It will be sent"), everyday words (use, help, start — not utilise, facilitate, commence), numbers and dates exact, one idea per paragraph. Cut "I hope this email finds you well", "please do the needful", "kindly revert".

## 4. Tone
Polite and direct. Firm without blame for complaints: facts, impact, the fix you want, a deadline. For bad news: say it early, explain briefly, offer what you can.

## 5. Templates
- **Request**: ask → why → deadline → thanks.
- **Follow-up**: refer to the earlier message → what's pending → new date.
- **Complaint**: what happened (date, order/ref number) → what was promised → what you want (refund/replacement) → by when → attachments.
- **Proposal**: problem → proposed solution → cost and timeline → benefits → next step.
- **Report**: summary first, findings, recommendations, appendix.

## 6. Final check
Read it as the recipient: is the ask obvious within 10 seconds? Names, numbers, attachments right? Would you be comfortable if it were forwarded? Then send. For Indian English, keep it neutral and warm; avoid ALL CAPS and too many exclamation marks.
