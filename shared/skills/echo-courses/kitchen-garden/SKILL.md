# Kitchen garden

## 1. Start small and sunny
Watch where sunlight falls: most vegetables need 5–6 hours of direct sun; leafy greens and herbs manage with 3–4. Start with 6–10 pots, not 50.

## 2. Containers and soil
Pots or grow bags with drainage holes (12–18 inches deep for tomatoes/brinjal, 6–8 for greens). Potting mix: roughly 40% garden soil or cocopeat, 30% compost/vermicompost, 30% sand/perlite or more cocopeat. Terraces: check waterproofing and weight; raise pots on stands.

## 3. Easy plants for beginners
Coriander, mint, curry leaf, spinach/palak, methi, amaranth, green chillies, tomatoes, brinjal, okra (bhindi), beans, bottle gourd/ridge gourd on a trellis, lemongrass, tulsi.

## 4. Seasons (typical for South/Central India — check local advice)
Kharif (June–July sowing, monsoon): okra, gourds, beans, chillies. Rabi (Oct–Nov): tomatoes, spinach, methi, coriander, peas, cabbage, carrots. Summer (Feb–March): gourds, cucumbers, amaranth — with shade cloth in peak heat.

## 5. Watering
Check the soil with a finger: water when the top 1 inch is dry, deeply, morning or evening. Over-watering (yellow, drooping leaves, soggy soil) kills more plants than under-watering. Mulch with dry leaves to save water.

## 6. Compost at home
Two bins: kitchen greens (vegetable peels, tea leaves) + browns (dry leaves, cardboard) in equal parts, keep moist like a squeezed sponge, turn weekly; ready in 6–8 weeks. No meat, dairy or oil.

## 7. Pests and problems (organic first)
Aphids/mealybugs: spray water, then neem oil (5 ml + a drop of soap in 1 litre water) in the evening. Yellow leaves: over-watering or nitrogen shortage — add compost. No fruit: not enough sun or poor pollination — shake tomato flowers gently. For serious crop problems, call the Kisan Call Centre (1800-180-1551).
