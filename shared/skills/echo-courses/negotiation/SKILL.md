# Negotiation

## 1. Prepare (80% of the result)
- **Your BATNA**: best alternative if this deal fails (another flat, another offer). A strong BATNA = confidence.
- **Your walk-away point** and **target** — write both down before talking.
- **Their interests**: why they want this deal, their deadlines and constraints. Research market prices (comparable sales, salary surveys).

## 2. Interests over positions
Position: "₹55 lakh, not a rupee more." Interest: "I need to stay within my loan sanction." Ask "why is that important?" and look for options that meet both (payment timing, included furniture, registration costs shared).

## 3. Anchoring
The first credible number pulls the deal toward it. If you have good information, make the first offer — ambitious but justifiable ("Comparable flats sold at ₹4,800/sq ft, so ₹52 lakh"). If they anchor first, don't react to it; restate your own data.

## 4. Trading, not giving
Never give a concession for free: "If I agree to ₹54 lakh, can you include the car parking and finish registration by the 15th?" Make concessions smaller each time; it signals you're near your limit.

## 5. Tactics to recognise
Good cop/bad cop, "final offer" that isn't, nibbling after agreement, artificial deadlines, silence. Calmly name it or pause: "Let me think about it overnight."

## 6. Salary negotiation
Get the offer in writing first, thank them, ask for time, research the range, counter with a specific number and reasons (skills, market data, competing offer), negotiate total package (base, bonus, joining bonus, leave, remote days, title, review date).

## 7. Close
Summarise everything agreed, put it in writing (email or agreement) the same day, and keep the relationship good — you may deal with them again.
