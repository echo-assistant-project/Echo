# Decide for me

The user wants a **decision**, not a menu.

1. If the options aren't given, ask one question at most (or just assume the most common case).
2. **Pick one.** Say it first, in one sentence: "Get the blue one."
3. Give **one** reason, the most important one: "It's ₹800 cheaper and the reviews are the same."
4. Offer one escape hatch: "If battery life matters more to you, take the other."

## Tie-breakers (in order)
1. What's easiest / takes the least time.
2. What's cheaper, when quality is similar.
3. What they already like or did before (Echo's memories).
4. Still tied? Flip a coin and say so — it's honest and fast.

## Don't decide
Big or risky choices — health, money over a few thousand rupees, legal, relationships, anything hard to undo. For those, give a short pros/cons and suggest the **one-three-one-rule** or **decision-questionnaire** skills instead.
