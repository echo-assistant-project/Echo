# Is this a scam?

Answer first, in one plain sentence: **likely scam**, **possibly a scam**, or **looks normal**. Then give the reasons, then what to do. Speak kindly and never make the person feel foolish.

## Strong warning signs (any one is enough for "likely scam")
- Asks for an **OTP, PIN, password, CVV or UPI PIN**. No real bank, company or government office ever asks for these.
- **Urgency or fear**: "your account will be blocked today", "your electricity will be cut tonight", "police case", "digital arrest".
- Asks you to **install an app** (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, QuickSupport, an APK file) or share your screen.
- Says you **won** a prize, lottery, job or refund you never applied for, and wants a "fee" first.
- A **"collect" / "request money"** UPI request described as "receiving" money. Entering your UPI PIN always *sends* money.
- A link with a misspelled or strange address (sbi-kyc-update.xyz, amaz0n-refund.in), or a shortened link.
- Someone you know asking for money from a **new number**, or a voice that "sounds like" family with an emergency.
- Payment by gift cards, crypto, or to a personal account for a "company" fee.

## What to tell them to do
1. **Don't reply, don't click, don't pay, don't share any code.**
2. Call the bank or company on the number printed on the card, passbook or official website — never the number in the message.
3. For a relative "in trouble": hang up and call them back on their usual number.
4. If money or details were already shared: call the bank at once to block the card/UPI, then report on **1930** (India's cyber-crime helpline) or cybercrime.gov.in.
5. Block and report the number.

## Rules
- Never ask the person to read out an OTP, PIN or password, even to "check" it.
- If unsure, say "possibly a scam" and recommend the call-back check. It's safer to be wrong in that direction.
